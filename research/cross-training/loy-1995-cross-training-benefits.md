# Loy et al. 1995 — Cross-training benefits (classic review)

## Key findings

- Cross-training can contribute to overall fitness and may transfer some aerobic adaptations across modes, but **sport-specific training** remains superior for peak performance in a given sport.
- Particularly useful during **injury rehabilitation**, psychological fatigue, or when modulating impact load.
- Transfer of VO2max-related adaptations between modes is incomplete; specificity principles strengthen as athlete level rises.

## Limitations

- Older narrative review; methods and athlete populations less standardized than modern metas.
- Exact effect sizes less precise than contemporary systematic reviews.

## Planning implications

- Use cycling as **complementary** aerobic and recovery tool (matches athlete’s weekday ~5 km commute and optional extensions).
- When foot is irritable: shift aerobic minutes to easy bike while protecting run quality days for later.
- Never assume bike km equal run km for tissue adaptation or race-specific fitness.
