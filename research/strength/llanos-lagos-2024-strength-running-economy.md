# Llanos-Lagos et al. 2024 — Strength training and running economy

## Key findings

- Meta-analysis of strength methods in middle-/long-distance runners (moderately to highly trained cohorts across included trials).
- Programs typically **6–24 weeks**, **1–4 sessions/week**.
- **High-load** strength: small improvement in running economy across a wide speed range.
- **Combined** methods (e.g. strength + plyometric elements): moderate improvement in RE at assessed speeds.
- **Plyometric** training: small RE benefit especially at speeds **≤12 km/h**.
- Submaximal or isometric-only protocols did not show clear RE improvements vs controls in this analysis.
- Greater high-load RE benefits associated with higher speeds and higher VO2max in moderator analyses.

## Limitations

- Heterogeneous protocols; certainty moderate/low depending on method.
- Injury-prevention effects are a separate question (not the primary outcome here).

## Planning implications

- Keep **2×/week S&C** (Mon/Thu) as a performance investment, not optional fluff.
- Prefer progressive lower-limb strength; add light plyometrics when load allows.
- On heavy run weeks, keep S&C moderate (quality over exhausting volume) so it does not spoil easy-run honesty or foot recovery.
