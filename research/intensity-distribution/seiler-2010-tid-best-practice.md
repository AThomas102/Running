# Seiler 2010 — TID best practice

## Key findings

- Elite endurance athletes across sports often accumulate a large majority of training (~80% of sessions or similar metrics) at low intensity (below first lactate/ventilatory threshold).
- A smaller fraction is performed at high intensity (above second threshold); dedicated time in the moderate “threshold middle” is often limited in successful programs.
- Hard–easy organization and adequate recovery between hard sessions are central themes.
- The review synthesizes observational patterns from high-level athletes rather than a single RCT.

## Limitations

- Much evidence is descriptive/observational of elites; translation to recreational rebuild phases needs judgment.
- Exact % schemes vary by how intensity is measured (HR, pace, lactate, session goals).

## Planning implications

- Default: **most dedicated run km easy** (conversational / below LT1).
- Avoid filling the week with continuous “sort of hard” running (historical failure mode for this athlete).
- Keep hard days hard and scarce during rebuild; add quality only when volume is bedding in.
