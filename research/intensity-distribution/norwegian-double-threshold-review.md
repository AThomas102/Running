# Norwegian double-threshold method — review summary

## Key findings

- Review of elite Norwegian long-distance training descriptions (small n of elite athletes across included articles).
- Typical elite volumes cited in the order of **~120–180 km/week**; **~75–80%** low intensity.
- **2–4** anaerobic-threshold sessions per week in base periods, often as intervals; **double-threshold days** (AM + PM threshold) appear in successful elite practice.
- Higher-intensity (&gt;~97% HRmax) work 1–2×/week as short intervals/sprints; long supra-threshold intervals less common in deep base.
- Intensity tightly monitored (lactate and/or HR). Pre-comp: fewer pure LT sessions, more race-pace specificity.
- Bakken’s practitioner write-up emphasizes clustering threshold load, then easier days, and keeping easy runs easy.

## Limitations

- Elite case material — not a recipe for recreational rebuild after an ~87 km spike and residual tendon issues.
- Requires monitoring discipline (lactate/HR) that this athlete may not use daily.

## Planning implications

- **Do not** introduce double-threshold while rebuilding from ~40–50 km or with foot flare risk.
- When volume is stable (~80–100 km) and foot is quiet, LT density can rise **gradually** (single quality LT day first).
- If ever using double-threshold: keep other days very easy; treat it as an advanced tool, not a default.
