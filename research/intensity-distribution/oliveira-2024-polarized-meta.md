# Oliveira et al. 2024 — Polarized vs other TID (meta-analysis)

## Key findings

- 17 studies, n = 437 athletes comparing polarized (POL) TID to other intensity distributions.
- **VO2peak:** POL showed a small significant advantage (SMD ≈ 0.24); effect clearer in interventions **&lt;12 weeks** and in **highly trained** athletes.
- **Time-trial, time-to-exhaustion, and VT2/LT2 speed/power:** POL similar to other TIDs (no clear superiority).
- Certainty of evidence for VO2peak advantage rated high in the authors’ GRADE assessment; risk of bias often “some concerns.”

## Limitations

- Heterogeneous athletes and how TID was quantified.
- “Other TIDs” pools pyramidal, threshold-heavy, etc. — not always a clean POL vs PYR head-to-head in every outcome.

## Planning implications

- Prefer **mostly easy + limited quality** (POL-like or pyramidal with honest Z1) over threshold-heavy weeks during rebuild.
- Do not expect polarized alone to magically improve race times vs a well-built pyramidal plan with the same easy discipline.
- For a competitive amateur aiming high, either POL or PYR can work; **easy must stay easy** either way.
