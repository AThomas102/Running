# Casado et al. 2022 — Elite distance runners periodization & TID

## Key findings

- Systematic review of highly trained/elite distance runners (10 included articles).
- Typical pattern: **pyramidal TID** in preparatory/precompetitive periods (large Z1, meaningful Z2/LT work, some Z3) shifting toward a more **polarized** pattern in the competitive period (more race-pace/Z3, less Z2).
- Hard day–easy day structure commonly recommended.
- Continuous tempo / LT2 intervals and Z3 sessions both appear at least weekly in elite practice at high volumes (often &gt;100 km·wk⁻¹ in that population).
- Marathoners tend more pyramidal; middle-distance more polarized — event-specific nuance.

## Limitations

- Elite samples — volumes and tolerance far above Andrew’s current rebuild.
- Observational synthesis of published training descriptions; not a single controlled trial of periodization models.

## Planning implications

- **Base/rebuild now:** emphasize Z1 volume and foot tolerance; light quality (strides) before dense LT.
- **Later HM block:** increase LT/tempo and long-run durability (more pyramidal), then sharpen with more race-specific intensity and taper.
- After A-races: recovery/transition before the next build (aligns with repo periodization rules).
- Do not copy elite weekly km until sustainable mid/high volume is proven with honest easies.
