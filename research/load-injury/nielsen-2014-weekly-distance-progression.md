# Nielsen et al. 2014 — Weekly distance progression and injury type

## Key findings

- Prospective cohort of **874 novice runners** with GPS watches (~1 year); **202** running-related injuries.
- After sessions, runners classified by recent weekly distance progression: **&lt;10% or regression**, **10–30%**, **&gt;30%**.
- **All-injury** rates: no statistically significant difference across progression bands.
- **Distance-related injury cluster** (PFPS, ITBS, MTSS, gluteus medius, GT bursitis, TFL, patellar tendinopathy): elevated rate for **&gt;30%** progression vs **&lt;10%** (HR 1.59, 95% CI 0.96–2.66, P=.07).
- Patient-facing summary (JOSPT Perspectives): [10.2519/jospt.2014.0506](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2014.0506) — advises caution with **&gt;30%** increases over a ~2-week window; lowest injury rates in the **&lt;10%** band for that injury cluster.

## Limitations

- Novices, not high-mileage returning athletes; exploratory subgroup for injury types.
- Does not replace single-session spike analyses (Ramskov).

## Planning implications

- Prefer week-to-week (or 2-week) volume jumps **well under 30%** when building.
- Do not treat **exactly 10%** as mandatory or proven optimal (see also Fredette 2022; Buist GRONORUN).
- Pair with session-longest caps and symptom monitoring for foot-sensitive rebuilds.
