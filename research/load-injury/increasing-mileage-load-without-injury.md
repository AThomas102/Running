# Increasing mileage and load without (guaranteed) injury — what evidence supports

There is **no protocol that eliminates injury risk**. Best evidence gives **risk modulators**, not a safety certificate.

## Primary papers

### 1. Nielsen et al. 2014 — Excessive weekly progression (novice GPS cohort, n≈874)

- [JOSPT 2014;44(10):739–747](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2014.5164); patient summary: [JOSPT Perspectives](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2014.0506)
- Compared weekly distance change: **&lt;10% / regression**, **10–30%**, **&gt;30%** (over ~2 weeks before injury classification).
- **Overall** injury rate: no clear difference across groups.
- **Distance-related injuries** (e.g. PFPS, ITBS, MTSS, some hip/knee soft-tissue): higher rate when progression **&gt;30%** vs **&lt;10%** (HR ≈ 1.59; borderline).
- **Not** clearly linked to that progression pattern: plantar fasciitis, Achilles/calf, some bone stress — other training errors (pace, hills, sudden speed) may matter more.
- Practical takeaway: prefer **not** jumping weekly km by **&gt;30%** in a short window; **&lt;10%/week** is cautious but not proven universally superior.

### 2. Ramskov et al. (~5200 runners) — Single-session spikes

- Local card: [ramskov-2024-session-spikes-5200.md](ramskov-2024-session-spikes-5200.md)
- Stronger signal: session distance **&gt;10% above longest run in past 30 days** → higher overuse injury rate; large spikes worse.
- Week-to-week ratio / classic ACWR **not** the standout predictor in that analysis.
- Practical takeaway: grow the **long run** slowly; raise weekly total via **extra easy km / days** when needed.

### 3. Fredette et al. 2022 — Training parameters systematic review

- Local card: [fredette-2022-training-params-injury-review.md](fredette-2022-training-params-injury-review.md)
- Evidence linking specific % rules to injury is **mixed**; authors: **no universal progression prescription**.
- The folk **“10% rule” is not justified** as a proven law.

### 4. Buist et al. 2008 — GRONORUN RCT (novices, 10% graded vs steeper)

- Graded ~10%/week (13 weeks) vs standard steeper (8 weeks) → **similar** injury incidence (~21% both).
- Shows a strict 10% ladder is **not** a magic shield in novices racing a short event.

## Operational rules (for this athlete)

1. **One stressor at a time** when possible: either bump weekly volume **or** add intensity (strides → tempo → intervals), not both big jumps same week.
2. **Cap session spikes:** next long ≈ **≤110%** of longest in last ~30 days (Ramskov).
3. **Cap week spikes:** avoid **&gt;~30%** week-to-week jumps after a stable week (Nielsen distance-injury signal); ~10–15% is a useful default when foot is quiet.
4. **Deload every 3–4 build weeks** (cut ~20–30% volume) — clinical/coaching practice that matches “adaptation needs recovery,” even where RCTs on % rules are weak.
5. **Keep easy easy** — historical failure mode for this athlete; intensity creep raises load without showing up as “km.”
6. **Monitor:** morning foot feel, in-run warm-through vs worsening, delayed numbness, easy HR drift — cut or hold before “pushing through.”
7. **Strength 2×/week** and bike for aerobic filler without impact (support load capacity).

## What this does *not* mean

- You cannot “research away” injury risk to zero.
- Residual foot sensitivity means treat the above as **ceilings**, and prefer the lower end when symptoms climb.
