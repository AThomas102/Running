# Half-/marathon training volume and longest run — performance vs injury

## Key findings

- Observational analysis of (half-)marathon trainees relating training characteristics to finish time, pace decline, and new running-related injuries.
- **Half marathon:** training volume **>32 km/week** and longest endurance run **>21 km** associated with faster finish and less pace decline (multivariable).
- **Marathon:** higher volume (e.g. >65 km/week) and avoiding a very short longest run associated with better finish times.
- **Injuries:** none of the examined training variables (including volume and longest run) were significantly associated with **new** RRIs in this study.

## Limitations

- Observational; self-selected race population; does not test “two near-max sessions in one week” vs “one long + shorter support days.”
- Longest-run thresholds for HM performance (~21 km) are later-block targets — not a prescription for early rebuild weeks.

## Planning implications

- Keeping **one clear weekly long** and building **total weekly km** supports HM performance later; need not invent two “longs” per week now.
- Absence of injury association here does **not** override session-spike evidence (Ramskov) or athlete-specific foot history.
- Near term: progress weekly volume; grow the single long gradually toward ~21+ km later in the HM block — not this week.
