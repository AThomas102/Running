# Ramskov et al. — Session spikes vs recent longest (n≈5200)

## Key findings

- Large prospective cohort of adult runners using device-logged distance (~18 months).
- Three exposures tested: (1) session distance vs **longest run in past 30 days**; (2) acute:chronic workload ratio (week vs prior 3 weeks); (3) week-to-week distance ratio.
- **Spikes in single-session distance** relative to the past-30-day longest were associated with higher overuse injury rates; risk rose further with larger spikes (including when distance more than doubled).
- Progressions up to ~10% of recent longest are **not automatically “safe”** — still carry some risk vs regression/tiny change.
- Spikes in ACWR and week-to-week ratio were **not** associated with increased injury rate in this analysis (contrasting some other sports ACWR literature).

## Limitations

- Observational; Garmin-using population may not generalize to all runners.
- Does not capture intensity, surfaces, or strength status fully.

## Planning implications

- Cap long-run increases: keep planned long ≈ **≤110% of longest in last ~30 days** unless intentionally progressing very carefully with quiet foot.
- After a 15.4 km long, do **not** jump to 18–20+ km the next week.
- Week total can rise via extra easy days without spiking the single longest session — or hold long and add midweek km carefully.
- Andrew’s preference for fewer run days still requires watching **per-session** load, not only weekly sum.
