# Nielsen et al. 2012 — Training errors and RRI

## Key findings

- Systematic review of training characteristics (volume, duration, frequency, intensity) vs running-related injury.
- **Frequency:** Apparent U-shape in crude analyses (very low or 6–7 days/week higher risk in some studies), but in key cohorts (**Macera, Walter**) excess risk of high frequency **disappeared after controlling for weekly volume**. Cumulative distance often the better marker than “lack of rest between runs.”
- **Longest run:** Walter et al. reported higher relative risk when longest weekly run was **>5 miles (~8 km)** vs shorter longest runs — older cohort, limited generalisability to trained athletes already doing 15+ km longs.
- **Volume:** Mixed; some link higher weekly mileage to injury, others show relative risk per mile falling as mileage rises (experience / selection).
- Authors conclude it is **not possible** to name a single “correct” frequency or declare specific training errors with certainty from the then-available evidence.

## Limitations

- Mostly observational; heterogeneous injury definitions; many self-report exposures.
- Predates large GPS-cohort session-spike analyses (e.g. Ramskov).

## Planning implications

- **4 run days/week is a normal, evidence-compatible pattern** for a trained athlete — not inherently unsafe vs 5–6 days.
- Do not assume “split everything shorter” is required for safety; volume and **per-session** load matter more.
- Still respect recovery between hard tissue stresses (especially with residual foot symptoms).
