# Fredette et al. 2022 — Training parameters and running injury

## Key findings

- Systematic review of associations between training parameters and running-related injuries.
- Evidence is mixed: some studies link higher weekly distance to more injury; others suggest higher distance can be protective in certain cohorts.
- Authors conclude **no universal recommendations** on training progressions can be issued from current evidence.
- The popular **“10% rule” for increasing weekly distance is not justified** by the reviewed evidence.

## Limitations

- Heterogeneous definitions of injury and training exposure.
- Mostly observational associations — causality limited.

## Planning implications

- Do not treat “+10% week” as a safety certificate or as a hard ceiling.
- Still avoid **large sudden spikes** (athlete’s own ~87 km week → crash is clinical history).
- Prefer multiple signals: foot symptoms, easy-pace honesty, recent longest run, sleep/fatigue — not a single % rule.
